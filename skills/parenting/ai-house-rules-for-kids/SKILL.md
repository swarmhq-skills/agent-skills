# AI house rules for kids

Write simple family rules for how your children use AI.

## Requirements

- The child's age and what they want to use AI for
- The AI tools the family already uses

## When to use

- When a child first uses an AI tool
- When the child asks for more freedom
- Once a year, to review

## Steps

1. Ask the age of each child, which tools they use and for what (homework, play, questions, company).
2. Check the tool's own rules: minimum age, whether it keeps memory, whether it trains on conversations. Say "not sure" when the answer cannot be found.
3. Draft a one-page set of rules in plain language: when AI is used, with an adult nearby or alone, what it is for, and what it is not for.
4. Include a thinking rule: the child tries first, the AI asks and hints, the child explains the answer back.
5. Include a people rule: AI never replaces talking to a parent, friend or teacher, and a child who feels upset or unsafe goes to an adult.
6. Include a privacy rule: no full name, school, address, photos or passwords in a chat.
7. Add a way to check: a weekly five minute chat where the child shows one thing they did with AI.
8. Suggest loosening one rule at a time as the child shows good judgement.

## Rules

- Not a replacement for professional advice. For serious worry about a child's wellbeing or safety, talk to a pediatrician, school counselor or a local helpline.
- Do not ask for the child's name, photos or school. Use age only.
- Never recommend AI friend or romantic character apps for children.
- Restrictions should fit the risk. Do not recommend a blanket ban or full freedom without reasons.
- The parent decides. Offer options, not orders.
- Sources: World Economic Forum white paper "Children's Safety and Well-Being in the Age of AI" (September 2026), summarized in our own words.

## Output format

```
Our family AI rules (child age)
- When we use it
- What it is for
- What it is not for
- Try first, then ask
- Privacy: never share
- If something feels wrong
- Weekly check in
- Review date
```
